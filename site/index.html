What this is

A demo of time-series forecasting for air raid alerts in Ukraine, per district (raion) and per oblast. The models answer: how likely is a new alert to start here in the next hour / 3 hours? They also estimate how long it would last, how many alerts and hours under alert to expect in the next 24 hours, whether an alert is likely to be red (missiles, ballistic, massed attacks) or yellow (drones), and in which 3-hour blocks (Kyiv time) of the next 7 days alerts are most likely.

The site is built to update the forecast every hour from the alerts.in.ua API (it is made for the full hour and appears on the site a few minutes later). Hourly updates are currently paused to stay within the hosting plan; the header shows when the forecast was made, and a banner says so when it is older than two hours. The 1-hour and 3-hour chances, durations, the 24-hour ranges and red/yellow are recomputed every hour; the 3-hour blocks of the week ahead are recomputed at 00:00 and 12:00 Kyiv time. The models are retrained about twice a month. The "Replay" page shows the four test weeks hour by hour; the history strip in the side panel shows the site's own forecasts over the last 7 days; the "Forecast check" page compares saved forecasts with what really happened.

How it works

The training data is a table with one row per district per hour. Each row holds what was known at that hour and what happened afterwards. A gradient-boosted decision-tree model (LightGBM) learns from millions of such rows which situations led to alerts. Inputs, all computed only from data before the forecast time:

  • This district now: is an alert active, hours since the last alert started and ended.
  • Recent activity: alerts and hours under alert in the last 1, 3, 6, 24 hours and 7 days.
  • Habits: how often an alert started at this clock hour on recent days; share of recent nights and days with alerts.
  • Its oblast and the whole country: share of districts under alert now, recent alerts nearby and nationwide.
  • Neighbouring districts (added 13 Oct 2026): the districts that share a border, also across oblast borders: is an alert running there, and how many new alerts started there in the last 1, 3 and 24 hours. They improved every forecast on the validation weeks.
  • Calendar: hour and weekday in Kyiv time; which oblast it is.

The model's settings were tuned on validation weeks before the test. Durations and the 24-hour numbers are shown as ranges (the model's 25th–75th percentile), because single numbers would suggest more precision than there is.

The week ahead blends two forecasts: the model, which knows what is happening right now, and a simple "pattern rule" (in how many of the last 14 days did an alert start in this 3-hour block?). The model's weight starts at 100% and fades to 0% by day 3, because current conditions say little about next week. The blocks are made at 00:00 and 12:00, so in between they can be older than the hourly numbers; the side panel marks where they overlap.

Under alert right now. A district that is under alert at the forecast moment is striped red on the map. Its chances are for a new alert after the current one ends. If one alert for the whole district has been running for 24 hours or more (this happens in front-line areas), the district is shown as a continuous alert and no forecast is given.

Local alerts. An alert for one community (hromada) or city counts only for that community, not for its whole district or oblast. Some front-line communities stay under one alert for months (e.g. Lypetska hromada near Kharkiv). Such alerts are shown as a note in the side panel, but they never make the whole district "under alert". (Until 13 Oct 2026 they did; that made Kharkivskyi district look permanently under alert. Fixed and the models retrained.)

Red or yellow uses a simple rule: the district's share of red alerts over the last 7 days. It predicted the level better than the machine-learning models, which had only a few weeks of data with levels to learn from (levels exist since 6 Sep 2026). The side panel shows how many alerts the share is based on. When all of them were yellow (or all red), it shows the count, e.g. "0 of 3", instead of a 0% or 100% that a handful of alerts cannot support.

The no-leakage rule

A forecast made at time t uses only information published before t. An alert still running at t is known only as "started, still active"; its end is not used. Models are trained on the past and scored on later weeks they never saw.

Data

  • Official alerts 2022-03 → 2026-09-07: Vadimkin/ukrainian-air-raid-sirens-dataset (parsed from the @air_alert_ua Telegram channel; artillery and other non-air-raid warnings removed).
  • Alerts since 2026-09-06 with yellow/red levels: JustJimer/ukraine-air-alert-stats (daily archive of the alerts.in.ua API).
  • Live alerts for the hourly forecast: the alerts.in.ua API.
  • Threat context: the public Telegram channels @war_monitor and @kpszsu (Air Force), read via their public web previews. Both were tested as extra inputs; neither improved the forecasts on the validation weeks, so the map does not use them (see "How good is it?").
  • District boundaries: OCHA administrative boundaries (via the Vadimkin repository); they also define which districts are neighbours. Map library: Leaflet.
  • Corrections (13 Oct 2026): a 105-hour outage of the official data (29 Aug – 2 Sep 2026, while volunteer sources kept reporting alerts) is treated as unknown and not used; two alerts in Zakarpattia whose end message was lost (Nov 2025, recorded as 122 hours) are ended with the alerts they started with. Every correction is listed in the repository (data/fixes/, reports/data_quality.md).

Data versions: the test scores use Vadimkin dataset commit bf0c323c and JustJimer archive commit 7abc556b (data up to 11 Oct 2026); these are fixed. The models behind the map were last trained on data up to – (Kyiv time); the exact data versions of each training are recorded in models/meta.json in the repository. Code, method and all decisions: github.com/yehor-o/time_series_air_alerts.
